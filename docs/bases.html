Bases of the Hecke algebras of the exceptional complex reflection groups

Companion to the paper The BMM symmetrising trace conjecture for all exceptional complex reflection groups and its programs. This page shows, for each non-real exceptional group W, the basis of the generic Hecke algebra used in the proof. Everything on it is read from the programs' files.

How to read this page

Every basis consists of braid lifts Tw of words w in the generators of W, numbered as in the paper (Figure 2) and in the programs. Each generator has a colour, used both for letters and for the edges of trees.

A bar denotes an inverse: . The letter z is the central element listed for each group, and on dashed edges the letter is an inverse. Long repeated factors are compressed: .

A coset tree lists one word for each coset of a subgroup U: its root is the empty word 1, and each node is the word of its parent followed by the label of the edge. The words are numbered x1 = 1, x2, … in the order of the programs, and node i stands for xi (or wi, yi, as the tree is named). Click a node, or a row of the word list, to see its word and its path from the root. Each tree is drawn whole, fitted to the window, top down or left to right, whichever shows it larger (a button switches); “Full size” enlarges it. The largest trees (those of G33 and G34) cannot be read when fitted: they open at full size, with a map of the whole tree in the corner (click or drag on it to move), and “Whole tree” fits them to the window. Below is the coset tree of U = ⟨s1⟩ in G4 (the example of the paper), next to the coset graph from which it is cut out: there, an arrow x → y labelled s means Ux·s = Uy, and the tree keeps exactly one arrow into each coset.

The groups at a glance

For G4, …, G29 the basis is a zk-basis B = {zkj sm Tw : 0 ≤ j < d, 0 ≤ m < es, w ∈ 𝒯}, where 𝒯 is a coset tree of Wk = ⟨s⟩⟨zk⟩. For G31, …, G34 it is built along a tower of parabolic subgroups, one coset tree per level. Classes of conjugate generators are grouped in braces, and as,0 is the constant term of the Hecke relation of s.
